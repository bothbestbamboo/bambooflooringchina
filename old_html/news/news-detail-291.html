Which Size of Plank Flooring Is Best for a Living Room: Small or Large?

Posted on 2024-10-28 8:07:36

Family, hello! We‘re going to discuss floor plank size today. Were you aware? Laying a floor is more complicated than just putting the floor down. It contains some amazing skills!


First, let‘s talk about the living room. The floor selection is important since it serves as the "front face" of the house. Why is the large plank floor in the living room a suitable choice? This is a significant query.

First of all, a large plank floor can provide the impression of space and grandeur. Imagine entering a living room and seeing a large, smooth floor with few joints. Do you think the room has suddenly gotten bigger? Larger plank floors can have fewer joints than smaller ones, which makes the floor appear smoother and more cohesive and gives people a feeling of ease and openness.

Second, a large plank floor can more effectively display style and quality. Large plank designs are frequently more opulent and sturdy, which can give the living room an air of grandeur. Large plank floors can be readily regulated and become the focal point of the living room‘s décor, regardless of whether it is a Chinese, European, or plain modern design.

So, how can large plank flooring be adequately paved? Here are detailed, useful advice for everyone.

1. Apply quality ground treatment
Make sure to completely treat the dirt before paving the floor. Verify that the ground is level, dry, and free of cracks. Cement mortar is a useful tool for leveling uneven land. In order to provide a solid base for the floor‘s paving, simultaneously clear the area of any dust or debris. To make sure the ground is dust-free and clean, you can carefully sweep it with a broom and then mop it with a wet mop.

2. Select an appropriate paving technique
Large plank flooring can be paved in a variety of methods, such as keel paving or suspended paving. Suspended paving is easy and practical. A keel is not necessary. A moisture-proof mat can be placed directly on top of the floor. This approach is quick to install, somewhat inexpensive, and appropriate for a comparatively level surface. Keel paving is more substantial and can significantly increase the floor‘s stability. Installing the keel is an option if the living room floor is not sufficiently level or if high floor stability is needed. Make sure the floor is level and firm after installation by paying attention to the keel‘s spacing and levelness during installation.

3. Take note of the floor‘s splicing.
Splicing large plank floors requires greater caution. Ensure that the floor is spliced tightly and smoothly. To increase the floor‘s splicing strength, you can employ locking technology or professional floor glue. To ensure a snug fit while splicing, line the floor‘s edges and lightly tap. At the same time, pay attention to the floor‘s direction and make an effort to maintain a consistent floor texture to enhance the living room‘s aesthetic appeal. People will see the floor as messy if the texture is uneven.

4. Set aside joints for expansion
When paving large plank floors, expansion joints must be kept aside due to the expansion and contraction properties of wood. The local climate and the floor‘s composition should be taken into consideration when determining the expansion joint‘s size. The expansion joint‘s breadth is typically between 8 and 12 mm. By reserving expansion joints, issues like arching and floor deformation during use can be prevented. You can leave the proper expansion joints around the wall and columns when laying the floor, and then cover them with closing strips or skirting.

5. Perform quality maintenance
Even once the floor is installed, proper upkeep is still required. Avoid using a mop that is too wet and clean the floor on a regular basis. To increase the floor‘s lifespan, you can employ expert floor cleaners and maintenance products. Avoid letting heavy things fall on the floor at the same time to prevent floor damage. If a liquid spill occurs on the floor, clean it up quickly to stop the liquid from soaking in and distorting the floor.

In summary, the living room should have a large plank floor because the floor laying abilities are superb. You may improve the aesthetics and comfort of your living room as long as you learn the proper installation techniques and upkeep techniques. Members of the family, hurry! Together, let‘s design a cozy and welcoming house.

What benefits can large plank floors offer? Small plank floors lose where?

First, let‘s discuss the benefits of large plank flooring.

First, large plank floors can produce a grandiose appearance. Imagine entering the living room and seeing a large, plank floor in front of you. There are almost any joints, so the room appears airy and light right away, making people feel at ease and open. Because small plank floors contain more joints, they may appear disjointed and claustrophobic to onlookers.

Second, large plank floors can more effectively display style and quality. Larger plank designs are frequently more stable and atmospheric. Big plank floors are readily regulated, making them the focal point of living room décor and raising the standard of the entire area, regardless of the style—simple modern, European classical, or Chinese. Small plank floors don‘t have the same striking visual impact and are somewhat commonplace.

Third, installing large plank floors is comparatively simpler. A certain amount of installation costs can be reduced because of the faster installation speed and fewer splicing times. Smaller plank floors are more difficult to install and need more splicing labor.

Big plank floors do have certain drawbacks, though.

On the one hand, large plank floors necessitate a more level surface. Large plank floors can easily become uneven after installation if the ground is not level, which will impact both use and look. Small plank floors are less demanding on the levelness of the ground and are more accommodating.

However, large plank floors are more challenging to move and carry. More labor and material resources are needed throughout the transportation process because of their greater size and weight. Smaller plank floors are easier to transport and weigh less.

Let‘s examine the benefits of small plank flooring.

For starters, tiny plank floors are reasonably priced. Families on a tight budget could consider tiny plank floors.

Second, there is more flexibility with small plank floors. In order to adapt to a variety of complicated terrains, they can be flexibly spliced according to diverse space shapes and sizes. Certain irregular spaces might not be a good fit for large plank floors.

Lastly, it is simpler to replace tiny plank floors that are damaged. It is less expensive to replace just the damaged tiny floor. Large plank floors are more difficult and expensive to replace once they are damaged.

Of course, there are drawbacks to small plank floors as well.

The most obvious is that large plank floors create a more atmospheric visual impression. The joints have a lot of gaps, making it easier to conceal dirt and more difficult to clean.

In summary, each type of plank flooring—large and small—has pros and cons of its own. You can make a decision based on your budget and actual needs.
