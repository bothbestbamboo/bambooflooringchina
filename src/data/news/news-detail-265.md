<p><span style="font-family: Verdana;"><span style="font-size: 12pt;">Stevia Leaf Extract refers to a natural sweetener derived from the leaves of the Stevia rebaudiana plant. It is widely used as a sugar substitute because it provides sweetness without significantly affecting blood sugar levels or adding calories. The key active components responsible for its sweetness are steviol glycosides.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Total Steviol Glycosides</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Steviol glycosides are the compounds extracted from the stevia plant that give it its sweet taste. The most common types of glycosides in stevia include:</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Stevioside</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Rebaudioside A, B, C, D, F</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Total Steviol Glycosides refers to the total amount of these glycosides present in the extract. The higher the concentration of steviol glycosides, the sweeter the extract.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
How to Use Stevia Leaf Extract (Total Steviol Glycosides) in Food</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
1.As a Sweetener in Beverages:</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Stevia is often used to sweeten beverages like tea, coffee, lemonade, smoothies, and even cocktails. A small amount can replace sugar since it is much sweeter.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Use sparingly (start with 1-2 drops or 1/4 teaspoon of powder) as it can be up to 200–300 times sweeter than sugar.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
2.In Baked Goods:</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Stevia can be used in baking, though it may not provide the same texture as sugar (since sugar contributes moisture and bulk).</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
For baking, consider using a blend of stevia with other sweeteners or bulking agents (like erythritol or coconut sugar).</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Common ratio: Use 1 teaspoon of stevia for every cup of sugar you would typically use, but adjust based on the sweetness level of the extract you have.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
3.In Sauces, Dressings, and Condiments:</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Stevia can be used to sweeten salad dressings, sauces (like BBQ sauce or ketchup), and even jams without adding the calories of sugar.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Start by adding small amounts and adjust to taste.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
4.In Desserts:</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
It works well in low-calorie desserts such as pudding, custards, yogurt, and ice cream.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Combine with other flavors to mask any potential aftertaste, which can occur with some stevia extracts.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
5.In Dairy and Non-Dairy Products:</span><br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Stevia can be used in yogurt, flavored milk, and plant-based milk alternatives to reduce sugar content.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Considerations When Using Stevia:</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Concentrated Sweetness: It is much more potent than sugar, so very small quantities are needed.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Aftertaste: Some people perceive a slight licorice-like aftertaste, depending on the concentration and purity of the stevia extract.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Heat Stability: Stevia is stable at high temperatures, making it suitable for cooking and baking.</span><br>
<br>
<span style="font-size: 12pt;">
Blending: To improve taste and texture, it is often combined with other sweeteners like erythritol or monk fruit.</span><br>

However, its concentrated nature means it should be used carefully to avoid over-sweetening or aftertaste issues.</span></span><br></p>
